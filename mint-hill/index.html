How Mint Hill Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year in hard-water areas.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular service, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or moisture around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap after a leak.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a flooded utility room.
