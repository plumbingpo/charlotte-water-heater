Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Indian Trail

Many early failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually encodes the manufacture date, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that range and also shows rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
