Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Higher gas or electric bills for less hot water
  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs
  • Water leaking from the bottom or body of the tank
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • Banging and popping from a tank packed with sediment
  • Repairs that keep coming back, one part after another
  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

A water heater swap seems easy right up until you count the connections. Gas units need a leak-free connection with a sediment trap, a correctly sized and pitched vent that sends carbon monoxide outdoors, and enough combustion air. Electric units need a breaker and circuit that match the element wattage. Every tank needs a correctly rated relief valve with a discharge pipe routed to a safe spot, and many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps under local code. Moving to tankless or a heat pump raises the stakes further, with larger gas lines, new vent materials, dedicated circuits, and condensate drains.

Professional service handles the full job, not just the tank. We size the heater by first-hour rating or flow rate, verify the fuel, venting, and electrical capacity, and add the expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs. Every gas and water joint is leak-tested, gas units get a draft and carbon monoxide check, and the old heater is hauled away. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. If your current tank still has good years left and just needs a part, we will tell you directly.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
  • Plan ahead: knowing the heater's age lets you replace it on your schedule, not after a leak
